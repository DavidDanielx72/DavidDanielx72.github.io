# SEMESTER 2: Industry Placement Agile Portfolio

## Context: How We Work at Suitable Focus

Suitable Focus is a startup agency that did not have formal project management infrastructure from day one. As the team grew and client work increased, we adopted Agile practices and brought in tools to keep us organised. Our current setup:

- **Zoho Projects:** Our central hub for logging tasks, tracking progress, managing milestones, and coordinating daily work across development and automation initiatives.
- **Zoho Cliq:** Daily communication channel for quick updates, blockers, and task coordination.
- **Google Meet:** Weekly and ad-hoc meetings for sprint planning, client updates, and retrospectives.

Our most recent client is **Milk and Cookies**, a new project where I am building automation systems from scratch to support their launch and client acquisition goals.

---

## 1. Agile Team Charter

### Evidence

Because Suitable Focus is a startup, we did not inherit a legacy team charter. We built our working agreements collaboratively as the team formed. The key elements are:

- **Shared Purpose:** Deliver reliable automation solutions and frontend integrations that help our clients launch and grow. For Milk and Cookies, this means building customer journey workflows that work from day one.
- **Core Values:**
  - Transparent communication through daily Zoho Cliq updates
  - Proactive problem-solving before escalation
  - Testing before deployment — especially for automation triggers that affect live client data
  - Respect for delivery timelines
- **Communication Channels:**
  - **Zoho Projects:** Task logging, milestone tracking, and sprint progress
  - **Zoho Cliq:** Daily stand-up updates, quick questions, and blocker escalation
  - **Google Meet:** Sprint planning, client syncs, and retrospectives
- **Operational Boundaries:**
  - All automation changes tested in a staging environment before going live
  - Blockers posted in Zoho Cliq within 24 hours
  - New client requirements discussed in Google Meet before being added to Zoho Projects
  - Frontend changes that affect live client pages require a second review before deployment

*[Insert Screenshot: Zoho Cliq channel description or a Google Meet screenshot from a team planning session]*

### Reflection (Collect, Select, Reflect, Connect)

**Collect:**
During my first weeks at Suitable Focus, I observed how the team communicated and where things slowed down. I noticed that without a formal system, tasks sometimes got lost between conversations. I gathered these observations from my own experience waiting for clarity on what to prioritise and from team discussions about how to improve.

**Select:**
From those observations, we selected agreements that directly addressed the gaps. Moving task tracking into Zoho Projects meant everyone could see what was in progress. Agreeing that blockers go into Zoho Cliq immediately meant we stopped waiting for weekly meetings to resolve small issues. These two changes had the biggest impact.

**Reflect:**
Having a shared team charter has made a real difference in a startup environment where things move fast. When a new client request comes in, we now have a process: discuss in Google Meet, log in Zoho Projects, prioritise against current sprint work. Before, those requests could sit in a chat thread for days. The charter gives us a reference point when priorities shift.

**Connect:**
The team charter connects directly to how we execute sprints. Because we agreed on communication protocols and tool usage upfront, sprint planning and daily updates run smoothly. This experience has taught me that even a small team benefits from clear agreements — and that a charter is a living document that we update as the team grows.

## 2. User Stories and Tasks

### Evidence

I write user stories from the end-user perspective for the automation workflows and frontend features I build for Milk and Cookies. These are discussed in Google Meet sessions with my supervisor and then broken down into tasks in Zoho Projects.

**User Story 1: Automated Customer Onboarding Journey**
> "As a new Milk and Cookies customer, I want to receive a welcome email with clear next steps immediately after signing up so that I know how to engage with the brand."

**Tasks:**
1. Set up webhook endpoint to receive form submission data
2. Map form fields to Mailchimp/HubSpot list and segments
3. Configure welcome email trigger with a short delay (5 minutes)
4. Test with sample submissions across different form types
5. Deploy to live and monitor delivery rates

**User Story 2: Post-Interaction Feedback Survey**
> "As a Milk and Cookies customer, I want to receive a short satisfaction survey after my interaction so that I can share feedback easily."

**Tasks:**
1. Design survey form with required fields
2. Connect survey to automation platform via webhook
3. Configure trigger to send 24 hours after interaction
4. Test data sync between survey tool and backend
5. Deploy and verify records update correctly

**User Story 3: Dynamic Landing Page Formatting (Frontend)**
> "As a site visitor, I want the Milk and Cookies landing page to display correctly regardless of the template's native styling limitations so that the design meets the brand's requirements."

**Tasks:**
1. Identify elements where the CMS template cannot render the required layout
2. Write custom client-side JavaScript to manipulate the DOM
3. Inject dynamic CSS rules to match the design specification
4. Format incoming JSON payloads and handle asynchronous UI state updates
5. Test across browsers and devices, then deploy

*[Insert Screenshot: Zoho Projects task list showing these user stories broken into tasks, OR your own markdown/notes file documenting the breakdown]*

### Reflection

Translating client requests into user stories has changed how I approach work. When Milk and Cookies asked for "automated customer journeys," that could have meant many things. Writing it as a user story forced me to clarify: who is the user, what do they need, and what is the outcome? That clarity made the task breakdown much easier.

The frontend workaround story is a good example of why tasks need to be specific. "Make the landing page look right" is not a task — it is a wish. Breaking it down into DOM manipulation, CSS injection, JSON formatting, and async state handling gave me a clear sequence to follow and a way to test each piece.

This approach also helps with scope. If a new request comes in mid-sprint, I can write it as a user story and add it to the backlog rather than trying to squeeze it into current work.

### Reflection

Managing the product backlog in a startup taught me how to balance competing priorities with limited time. There are always more tasks than hours, and the backlog is how we decide what matters.

I evaluate items based on:
- **Client impact:** Does this block Milk and Cookies from launching or operating?
- **Urgency:** Are there deadlines or dependencies?
- **Effort:** How much development time will this require?

Zoho Projects makes this visible to the whole team. When my supervisor asks what I am working on, I can point to the top of the backlog and explain the reasoning. It also helps when a client request arrives — I can add it to the backlog and discuss prioritisation rather than dropping current work.

## 4. Sprint Backlog

### Evidence

We work in one-week to two-week sprints. The sprint backlog is the subset of the product backlog we commit to for the current cycle. It is tracked in Zoho Projects and discussed in Google Meet at the start and end of each sprint.

|------|--------|-------------|

| Map form fields to Mailchimp/HubSpot | In Progress | Me |
| Test welcome email trigger conditions | To Do | Me |

| Review survey form validation bug | Done | Team Member |
| Fix survey validation bug | In Progress | Team Member |
| Update mobile layout CSS for survey portal | To Do | Team Member |

*[Insert Screenshot: Zoho Projects sprint view showing tasks in To Do, In Progress, and Done]*

### Reflection

The sprint backlog makes a project like Milk and Cookies feel manageable. Instead of looking at everything that needs to be done for launch, I focus on the specific tasks for this week.

When unexpected blockers arise, Zoho Projects helps the team re-evaluate. For example, if the JavaScript DOM fixes take longer than expected, we can see the impact on other tasks and adjust during our next Google Meet check-in. Because everyone can see the board, there is less need for status meetings — the information is already there.

I have also learned to estimate better. In the beginning, I underestimated how long testing automation triggers would take. Now I include testing time in my estimates, which makes sprints more realistic. This is especially important for automation work, because a trigger that fires incorrectly can affect real customers.

## 5. Agile Logbook

### Evidence

I maintain a daily logbook that records my work progress, blockers, and reflections. It combines my personal notes with the daily updates I post in Zoho Cliq. Here is an excerpt from a week working on Milk and Cookies automation:

**Week 1 - Monday**
- **Stand-up notes:** Started setting up webhook endpoint for onboarding automation. Waiting for Mailchimp API credentials.
- **Tasks completed:** Reviewed documentation for Mailchimp/HubSpot integration
- **Blockers:** API credentials not yet received
- **Hours:** 6

**Week 1 - Tuesday**
- **Stand-up notes:** Received API credentials via Zoho Cliq. Testing webhook connection.
- **Tasks completed:** Webhook endpoint created, initial connection test successful
- **Blockers:** None
- **Hours:** 8

**Week 1 - Wednesday**
- **Stand-up notes:** Mapping form fields. Found issue with date format coming through the webhook.
- **Tasks completed:** Field mapping complete, date format bug fixed with a small JavaScript transform
- **Blockers:** Need to test with a live form submission
- **Hours:** 7

**Week 1 - Thursday**
- **Stand-up notes:** Testing welcome email trigger. Emails not sending on first attempt.
- **Tasks completed:** Identified issue - trigger delay was set to 0 seconds
- **Blockers:** Resolved - changed delay to 5 minutes
- **Hours:** 8

**Week 1 - Friday**
- **Stand-up notes:** Onboarding automation deployed to staging. Requesting review. Also started JavaScript DOM fixes for landing page.
- **Tasks completed:** Staging deployment complete, team review requested; initial JS fix committed
- **Blockers:** Waiting for review before production deployment
- **Hours:** 6

**Retrospective Notes (End of Week):**
- What went well: Onboarding workflow works correctly in staging
- What could improve: Should have tested trigger conditions earlier to catch the delay issue
- Action for next sprint: Add more thorough testing before marking automation tasks as "done"

### Reflection

Keeping an Agile logbook has been one of the most useful habits I have developed during my internship. Documenting my daily progress forces me to reflect on what I actually accomplished versus what I planned.

Over time, I have noticed patterns:
- I underestimate testing time, especially for automation triggers that affect live data
- I work better in the morning on complex technical tasks like JavaScript DOM manipulation
- Blockers that seem urgent often resolve quickly once I communicate them in Zoho Cliq

The logbook also helps during retrospectives. Instead of relying on memory, I can review my notes and give specific examples. For the trigger delay issue, I documented the cause and the fix, so next time I set up an automation I check the delay setting first.

From a technical perspective, recording troubleshooting steps has helped me avoid repeating mistakes. When I encountered the date format issue in the webhook data, I documented the JavaScript transform I used to fix it. That note has already helped me with a similar issue on a different form.
